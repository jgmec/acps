This is a model, not a measurement. Each cell's position is Apple's estimate of where the cell is used, not the tower location. Signal is modelled as falling off with distance (path-loss exponent: about 2 in open country, 3–4 in towns), and a phone is assumed to use whichever cell of its operator is strongest, allowing for random signal variation from buildings and terrain. Serving likelihood is the chance this cell is the strongest at a spot. Relative signal is this cell's modelled level above the edge of coverage, ignoring other cells. Accuracy weight blurs each cell's position by its accuracy; at 0 positions are taken as exact. Max range is the distance at which signal is assumed to fade out; it starts from 4× the typical spacing between nearby cells. The experimental field 12 option fades this cell out at its own reach instead: an undocumented number from Apple's reply that grows with the spacing between cells like a coverage radius, though its meaning is a guess. Operators run several frequency layers over the same area and a phone doesn't choose between layers by distance, so by default this cell only competes with cells on its own frequency (EARFCN); cells whose frequency isn't known yet are left out until the crawler sees them again. Cells Apple doesn't know, or that the crawler hasn't found yet, are missing from the model.
